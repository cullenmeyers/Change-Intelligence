HubSpot's 25,000-record limit: clean up, buy capacity, upgrade, or switch?

Reviewed November 25, 2026 Primary evidence: HubSpot product catalog + data-limit docs Audience: HubSpot Starter users on flexible seats-and-credits pricing
Short answer Do not migrate just because you are approaching 25,000 records. First confirm what is consuming your capacity. HubSpot's current catalog lists 25,000 records for Starter, 250,000 for Professional, and 15,000,000 for Enterprise under flexible seats-and-credits pricing, and HubSpot also offers record-capacity increases. If the rest of HubSpot still fits, cleanup or added capacity may be cheaper and safer than a migration.

What changed?

HubSpot's flexible seats-and-credits pricing uses a shared record capacity for applicable CRM object types. Under the current catalog, Starter includes 25,000 HubSpot records. Marketing Contacts are included as a record type within that capacity instead of being priced separately under this model.

HubSpot says customers can increase record capacity with a Basic or Scaled Records Limit Increase. That means the real decision is not simply “upgrade or leave.” You have four practical paths.

The four paths

PathBest whenMain risk
1. Clean upYour limit is being consumed by data you no longer need, duplicates, or records that do not deserve to stay in the live CRM.Deleting useful history just to save space can hurt reporting and future context.
2. Buy record capacityHubSpot still fits your workflow and the capacity add-on costs less than migration disruption.You may only postpone the same decision if record growth stays high.
3. UpgradeYou also need Professional/Enterprise capabilities, not just more records.Paying for a much larger plan only to solve storage can be expensive.
4. Switch CRMThe record limit is one of several reasons HubSpot no longer fits: price, workflow complexity, missing integrations, adoption, or support.Migration can lose history, associations, automation logic, or user adoption.

Step 1: find what is actually consuming the limit

Before comparing alternatives, open HubSpot's data model/limits view and inspect current usage. HubSpot's documentation says the Limits tab is where accounts can track CRM-data limits such as records, associations, custom properties, pipelines, and related capacity.

  1. Check your account's current record usage and growth rate.
  2. Separate data you actively use from data kept only for historical reference.
  3. Estimate how quickly you will hit 25,000 if nothing changes.
  4. Price the record-capacity increase and the plan upgrade before pricing a migration.

When staying on HubSpot makes sense

Staying is usually the safer choice when your team already uses HubSpot consistently, the workflows are working, integrations are stable, and capacity is the only serious problem. A migration has real costs: cleanup, exports, field mapping, associations, automations, activity history, retraining, and a period where the team may trust the CRM less.

When switching becomes rational

The 25,000-record ceiling matters more when it exposes a broader mismatch. Switching deserves serious consideration when you are already unhappy with the price, users avoid the product, important workflows require workarounds, or you are paying for a platform much broader than the job you actually need done.

Do not start with “Which CRM is best?” Start with the requirement HubSpot is failing.

Migration test before you commit

Do not migrate the entire database first. Take a representative sample: a few companies, contacts, deals, custom fields, activity history, and one or two automations. Rebuild those in the candidate CRM and make the new system prove that it preserves the context your team relies on.

  1. Export a small representative sample from HubSpot.
  2. Map the fields and relationships into the candidate CRM.
  3. Recreate one important automation or follow-up workflow.
  4. Have the actual users work from the test system for several days.
  5. Compare total cost at your expected record and seat count 12 months from now.

Decision rule

Stay if the limit is the only real problem. If cleanup or added capacity solves the issue and your team likes HubSpot, migration risk may not be worth it. Switch only when the record limit is part of a larger product-fit or cost problem.
